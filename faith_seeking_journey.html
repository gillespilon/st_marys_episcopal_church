Chapter 1

Stephen has been teaching this course since 1986. He described his history and background. He asked if anyone had questions for him. “What is your greatest question about your faith?” His answer—what is the continuity of memory from this life to the next?

In each session some of the participants will introduce themselves and answer some of these question:

  • name
  • where are you from?
  • religious history
  • what brought your to the Episcopal Church?
  • what question do you have?

We should read one chapter of the book before each session.

Faith is like courage—most of the time you do not need it; in times of cris we need them but it is too late.

Religion is the currency of faith. It allows us to name and assign value, and share our faith with others.

Jesus tells us that fiath is seeking and all other things will fall in place.

There are no definitions of God in the Bible. There are no essay, no protraits. God is known by the poeple who “call upon his name.”

When we share our stories these are about us and our journey of faith, discipleship, etc. Our identify is our faith story. And stories reveal God to us.

God dwells in light; we live in shadowland (C.S. Lewis, “Farewell to Shadowlands” in The Chronicles of Narnia series; and Beyond the Shadowlands). How can we bridge ourselves to God? We look up, out, or within. Western philosophy and the development of wisdom is speculative. The other way is God comes to us. For example, Moses turned aside to see the burning bush and God saw this and spoke to him.
